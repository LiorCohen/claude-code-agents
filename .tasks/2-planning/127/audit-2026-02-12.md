## Recommendations

### High Priority

1. **Replace `.sort()` with `toSorted()`** across 6 files — straightforward find-and-replace. ES2023 `toSorted()` is available with the `ES2022` target + `lib` configuration, or polyfill if needed.

2. **Create barrel files for `lib/` and `types/`** — add `index.ts` to both directories with re-exports, then update imports throughout.

3. **Extract logic from `providers/index.ts`** — move provider state management and detection logic to a dedicated `providers/state.ts` or similar. Keep `index.ts` as pure re-exports.

### Low Priority

4. **Standardize Node.js import specifiers** — replace bare `'fs'` and `'path'` with `'node:fs'` and `'node:path'` in `cli.ts` and `lib/logger.ts`.

5. **Convert `settings/` imports to `@/` alias** — replace `'../types/settings'` with `'@/types/settings'` (6 occurrences).

6. **Resolve stdin `.push()` pattern** — consider `Readable.toArray()` or accumulate via `concat` in a functional pattern for the 2 hook files.

7. **Add `readonly` to `LocalConfigUrls.databases` inner object** in `env/config.ts`.

---

## Verdict

The codebase is in **good shape overall**. The prompt files are fully compliant with system-cli-standards — all invocations are canonical, authority boundaries are respected, and no broken references exist in scaffolded templates.

The TypeScript source has 40 findings, primarily in two categories: import organization (barrel files + path aliases) and mutable `.sort()` usage. None are correctness bugs — they're consistency and style issues per the typescript-standards specification. The high-priority items (`.sort()` → `toSorted()`, barrel files) are quick wins that bring the codebase into full compliance.
